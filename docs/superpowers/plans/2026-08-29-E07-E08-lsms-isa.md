# E07 / E08 — LSMS-ISA: the two terms with no data, and the outcome label there has never been

Two experiments against the same public dataset. **E08 is the more valuable and the harder** — it is
the first opportunity this project has had to validate anything against a real agronomic outcome.

## The dataset

[LSMS-ISA](https://microdata.worldbank.org/index.php/catalog/lsms) — World Bank national
longitudinal household surveys focused on agriculture. Countries: **Burkina Faso, Ethiopia, Malawi,
Mali, Niger, Nigeria, Tanzania, Uganda**. A [harmonised 2008–2024
panel](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12635345/) covers 200,000+ plot observations and
~59,000 households. Free, but **requires registration** on the World Bank microdata catalogue — it
cannot be auto-fetched, so acquisition is a manual first step, not a script.

**Where to get it (Nigeria GHS-Panel, the relevant country — Kaduna, Kano and Benue are Nigerian):**

| Wave | Years | Catalogue |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 4 | 2018–19 | [catalog/3557](https://microdata.worldbank.org/index.php/catalog/3557) |

The uniform release is already reconciled across waves 1–4 and removes most of the variable-mapping
work in E07's checklist below; prefer it over harmonising the per-wave files by hand.

**Coverage against this project's clusters:** Kaduna, Kano and Benue are Nigeria; Ethiopian_Highlands
and Tanzania_Morogoro have cluster definitions in code. **Kenya is not in LSMS-ISA** — `Kenya_Rift_Valley` has no counterpart.

## The constraint that shapes both experiments

Public LSMS-ISA releases **enumeration-area (EA) cluster coordinates, never household ones**, and
[displaces them](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304387822000839): rural clusters
by a random **0–5 km** (99%) or 0–10 km (1%), urban by 0–2 km, following the DHS method.

This project's satellite features are a **500 m box**. The displacement is **ten times the feature
footprint**, so the Sentinel pixel at a published coordinate is in general *not that farm's pixel*.

**Therefore: all satellite and weather signal must be aggregated to the EA scale.** Any analysis that
joins an LSMS-ISA household to a 500 m pixel and calls it that farm's canopy is measuring noise. The
error has been quantified — [*Privacy protection, measurement error, and the integration of remote
sensing and socioeconomic survey data*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.05220) — read it before designing
the join, not after.

This is the same resolution honesty already forced on this project once: a reanalysis cell of
unstated size, a 500 m satellite mean, and a per-farm advisory are three different scales.

# E07 — Do Exposure and Vulnerability behave sensibly on real households?

**The gap.** `Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability`, and **two of the three terms have never
been evaluated against any data**. The 4,596-row panel has **zero** farmer or household columns —
that data reaches the service only at serving time. Meanwhile the product ranks its triage queue by
`expected_loss = exposure × loss_rate`, so the ranking that decides who gets an extension visit
leans hardest on the term with nothing behind it.

**Note what this is not.** `assess_vulnerability` and `assess_exposure` are **not fitted** — they are
published-weight compositions. So E07 is not a training exercise. The question is whether their
*behaviour* on real household distributions is sensible, or whether the composition collapses,
saturates, or ranks perversely.

### Tasks
- [ ] Acquire the Nigeria GHS-Panel (and Ethiopia/Tanzania if cheap) household modules. Record the
      wave, the citation and the access date — this is external data and its provenance matters as
      much as the panel's.
- [ ] Map LSMS-ISA variables onto the `assess_vulnerability` signal names and the `assess_exposure`
      inputs (`area_ha`, `expected_yield_t_ha`, `price_per_t`). **Write the mapping down as a table
      with the source variable name** — a silent mismapping here is indistinguishable from a result.
- [ ] Run the **real** `domain.risk` functions over real household rows. Do not reimplement them.
- [ ] Report the distribution of vulnerability and exposure, and the spread of `risk_score` and
      `expected_loss` they induce for a fixed hazard. **The question is whether ranking by
      `expected_loss` produces a defensible triage order or is dominated by farm size.**
- [ ] **Confirm the protected-attribute guard fires** on real data carrying `head_gender` and
      `household_max_education`. This is the case it exists for.
- [ ] Report a fairness slice: does the induced ranking differ by household headship and landholding
      size? That is the disparate-impact measurement those attributes are retained for.

# E08 — Yield as the outcome label this project has never had

**Why this matters more than E07.** Every conclusion so far rests on a proxy: `forward_z`, a
peer-relative canopy anomaly. `docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` states plainly that it "says nothing about
yield, disease incidence, income, or whether a visit would have helped, and no outcome data exists
here to measure the gap." LSMS-ISA has **realised plot-level yields**. This is the T2 label the
roadmap says is a season away — available retrospectively, today.

### The question
Does the hazard term — computed from weather and satellite alone — carry information about
**realised yield anomaly** at the EA scale?

### Tasks
- [ ] Build an EA-level panel: for each LSMS-ISA EA and season, aggregate the same upstreams this
      project already uses (Open-Meteo daily, Sentinel-2/1) **over the EA's plausible extent, not a
      500 m box** — sized to the displacement radius, and state the choice.
- [ ] Compute hazard with the real `domain.risk.assess_hazard` and `domain.agronomy` functions.
- [ ] Target: yield anomaly relative to the EA's own history, or to the survey-year cohort. **Use the
      same within-transformation discipline E01–E05 established** — an absolute yield is dominated by
      a plot fixed effect exactly as `forward_z` was, and repeating that mistake with a new dataset
      would be unforgivable.
- [ ] Evaluate with the existing protocol: spatially blocked, forward-chained, net benefit with
      intervals, and the baselines. **Reuse `lab/eval`** rather than writing new metrics.
- [ ] Pre-register the reading before seeing the number, as E06 does.

### Threats to validity, stated now
- **Displacement** (above) is the dominant one, and it is not removable — only accommodated.
- **LSMS-ISA yields are largely self-reported**, with known area and production measurement error;
  crop-cut subsamples exist for some waves and are the better instrument where available. Treat a
  weak result as jointly a statement about the label's noise.
- **Population mismatch:** LSMS-ISA households are a national sample, not this product's registered
  farmers. A relationship here need not transfer to them.
- **Temporal overlap exists after all — but the file that would exploit it may not be released.**
  This bullet previously read "temporal overlap is narrow", written against Wave 4 (2018-19), which
  indeed predates the committed panel. **Wave 5 lands inside it:** post-planting July-September 2023,
  post-harvest January-March 2024, against a panel spanning 2022-09 to 2026-06. So the 2023 season is
  observed by both, and E08's yield label can be joined to seasons this project already has features
  for, instead of only to historical ones. Wave 5 is confirmed LSMS-ISA, collects crop-level harvest
  and GPS-measured plot area, and covers ~1,590 long-panel plus ~3,600 refresh households.
  **The catch:** the Wave 5 catalogue page lists its geovariables — the displaced EA coordinates —
  as *forthcoming*. That file is precisely what the EA-scale join below requires. **Confirm the Wave
  5 geovariables are downloadable before planning any spatial work on it**; without them Wave 5 is
  usable for E07 (household attributes, no coordinates needed) but not yet for E08. Waves 1-4 have
  released geovariables and remain the fallback for the spatial half.
- **Kenya has no counterpart**, so one of four clusters cannot participate.

## Sequencing

E07 first — it is cheaper, needs no satellite work, and immediately fills the two-thirds of the risk
composition that has never seen data. E08 is the more valuable result and should not be rushed
behind it.

Neither changes the serving path. Neither should touch `data/training_set.parquet`: follow the
`data/site_covariates.parquet` precedent and keep external data in its own site- or EA-keyed file, so
the 38 committed experiment provenance hashes stay valid.
